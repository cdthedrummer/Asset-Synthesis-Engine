## How to use this (read once, 60 seconds)

1. **Part A is done.** It's the instructions to the model. Don't touch it unless you want to change what you get back.  
2. **Part B is yours.** Every project you're involved in is already listed with a one-line description pulled from your own files. Your job is only to add the honest, unpolished truth under each.  
3. **Two lanes, on purpose.** Don't fill every field for every project — that's the fade-trap. Do the **Fast Lane** (a rating \+ one line) for *everything*. Then pick the **5–8 projects that actually matter** and do the **Deep Lane** for only those. The model does better analysis on 6 honest deep-dives than 25 shallow ones.  
4. **Don't self-censor.** Messy notes, cursing, "I have no idea," half-thoughts — all fuel. The whole point is finding the connections *you* can't see. Polished input \= generic output.  
5. **When done, paste the entire filled-out file into Claude Fable** (or back to me) as a single message.

# PART A — Instructions to the Model *(already written — leave as-is)*

You are my strategic partner for a portfolio audit. I'm handing you a year+ of work — professional, personal, dormant, and creative — and I need you to find the connective tissue I'm missing and tell me, bluntly, where to point my limited time.

**Who I am (calibrate to this):** I'm a strategist, ideator, connector, and marketer — not a coder or a solo executor. I'm strong on vision, relationships, packaging, and analytical skepticism. My known failure modes are analysis paralysis, doing the fun/easy work (branding, design, marketing) while avoiding the hard core (dev, distribution, the frog), starting strong and fading, and needing external accountability. Structure your advice for **delegation, not solo building.**

**How to talk to me — non-negotiable:**

- Direct, casual, evidence-based. No corporate tone, no hedging, no sappy filler.  
- **Commit to recommendations.** No "it depends." Pick a lane and defend it.  
- **Push back when I'm wrong.** If I'm re-litigating a settled decision for reassurance, or protecting a project I should kill, call it out by name.  
- Short and sharp over long and soft. Hemingway, not McKinsey.

**Deliver exactly these five things, in this order:**

**1\. The Difficulty × Upside Quadrant.** Plot *every* project on a 2×2. X-axis \= Difficulty to Complete/Ship (Low→High), factoring in MY specific gaps (I can't code, I need accountability). Y-axis \= Upside/Impact (Low→High). Label each project into one of four buckets:

- **Quick Wins** (low difficulty, high upside) — do now  
- **Big Bets** (high difficulty, high upside) — need a plan, a partner, or a budget  
- **Fillers** (low difficulty, low upside) — only if bored  
- **Thankless Tasks / Cutting-Room Floor** (high difficulty, low upside) — kill or park, and say so Give it to me as a **visual-first artifact** — an actual rendered 2×2 grid or clearly-drawn diagram, not just a text table. I'm a visual learner; make me see it. A backing table is fine as a supplement.

**2\. Categorization.** Group everything by *capability domain*, not by work-vs-personal. I suspect my job and my hobbies are secretly the same skill applied to different inputs — prove or disprove that. Name the domains (e.g. Automation/Scoring Rigor, Narrative/Storytelling, Gamified Self-Improvement, Community-Building, Physical Product). Show me which domains I over-index on and which I neglect.

**3\. The Shortcoming Audit.** For every stalled or unpublished project, diagnose *why* it stalled — and be specific about the category of failure: (a) missing engineering/execution, (b) missing packaging for an audience/leadership, (c) missing accountability, (d) genuine misalignment with what I care about, or (e) I did the easy 80% and skipped the hard 20%. Pattern-match across projects. Tell me the ONE structural fix that would unstick the most projects at once.

**4\. The Red Thread (3–5 paragraphs).** This is the point of the whole exercise. Explain how these projects — my ROI-scoring and prioritization work at Omnicom, my gamified quiz/goal apps, my sci-fi writing, my board-game-café dream, my product ideas — connect into a *single coherent career identity*. What's the through-line that turns 25 scattered efforts into one story I can tell (and sell) to colleagues, friends, and myself? Where are the "hidden bridges" — places where a skill I've already proven at work would rescue a personal project that's dying for exactly that skill, and vice versa? Be specific and name the bridges.

**5\. The Prioritized Task List (strict order of importance).** A ranked action list, not a menu. For each item say *what it is, why it's ranked there, and the first concrete move.* Explicitly cover:

- **Publish immediately:** what's closest to a shareable win I can put in front of people this month.  
- **Stop / kill:** what to intentionally leave on the cutting-room floor and why. Give me permission to quit things.  
- **Package:** how to repackage work I've already done so it's useful to friends, colleagues, and my own career narrative — turning "ideas" into "products/frameworks" I can share.  
- **The one accountability system** you'd bet on to fix my fade pattern, given who I am.

End with a **30-day plan** for the single highest-priority Quick Win — day-by-day or week-by-week, structured so I'm delegating/directing rather than solo-grinding.

Ask me clarifying questions *only* if something in Part B genuinely blocks the analysis. Otherwise, make your best call, state the assumption, and go.

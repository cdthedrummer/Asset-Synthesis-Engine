# CHARLIE’S PORTFOLIO OPERATING REVIEW
## Master intake document + analysis prompt for Claude/Fable or another advanced model

> **Purpose:** turn a large body of professional work, personal projects, dormant ideas, and life priorities into a decision-grade portfolio: what to lead, publish, delegate, merge, park, or kill.

## How to use this
1. Complete the **Frame** and the **Fast Pass** for everything. Do not overthink it.
2. Select only **5–8 items** for the Deep Dive.
3. Attach actual evidence: decks, screenshots, links, metrics, prototypes, feedback, and rough notes.
4. Paste this entire document and the supporting files into the model.
5. Do not start another project until the model returns a ranked portfolio and you choose the top three active bets.

## Non-negotiable decision rules
- At most **three active bets** at once: one career bet, one life/community bet, and one creative or commercial bet.
- Every active item needs a named audience, next proof point, owner, deadline, and distribution path.
- A project that depends on Charlie becoming a full-time coder requires delegation, a partner, or a kill/park decision.
- Career work must create visible evidence for executive scope, compensation, decision rights, or team ownership—not merely more workload.
- Personal technology must improve weekly life. A beautiful app Charlie does not use is not shipped; it is a prototype.
- Health, marriage, family, friendship, and community are portfolio outcomes—not side quests that must be monetized.

# PART A — INSTRUCTIONS TO THE MODEL

You are acting as my **Portfolio Strategist, Executive Career Architect, and ruthless operating partner**. I am giving you a year-plus of professional work, personal projects, dormant ideas, and life priorities. Your job is not to praise the volume. Your job is to identify the few things that deserve my time, show me the connective tissue, and turn them into a practical operating plan.

Calibrate to me: I am a strategist, marketer, product thinker, storyteller, and connector. I can prototype and learn technical tools, but my best work happens when I frame the problem, create the model, persuade stakeholders, and direct execution. My failure mode is doing the interesting 80%—concept, design, branding, prototype, narrative—then fading at the hard 20%: engineering, distribution, maintenance, political follow-through, adoption, or repetitive operations.

Be direct. Commit to recommendations. Do not give me a menu of equally weighted possibilities. Do not advise me to “find balance” without specifying what gets removed. Separate facts from inference. When evidence is weak, say so.

## Required output — exactly in this order

### 1. Brutal one-page verdict
Tell me what phase of my career/life I am in; the three truths I am avoiding; the sentence that should guide the next 12 months; and the three active bets I should carry.

### 2. Visual portfolio board
Render every item on Difficulty × Total Upside. Color by Do / Delegate or Partner / Park / Kill. Size markers by evidence or traction. Add a backing table with 0–100 scores for upside, difficulty, time to first proof, energy, strategic fit, career leverage, economic upside, life/community benefit, reuse of assets, dependency risk, and confidence.

### 3. Capability map and red thread
Group the portfolio by capability—not work versus personal. Identify proven strengths, over-investment, avoided capabilities, missing hires, and the bridges between projects. Create a concise career identity stronger than “the AI guy.”

### 4. Shortcoming audit
For every stalled, low-adoption, or unpublished item, diagnose the primary failure: execution/engineering, audience/distribution, packaging/merchandising, accountability/ownership, dependency/politics, genuine misalignment, or easy-80/hard-20 avoidance. Name the one structural change that rescues the most value.

### 5. Career acceleration plan
Build a specific path to executive scope and $300K+: flagship work, evidence leadership needs, work I should stop owning, team/decision rights to request, impact merchandising, promotion narrative, title territory, compensation case, and the next three executive conversations with concrete asks.

### 6. Personal portfolio plan
Treat local friendship/community, health, marriage/family, travel, home/location, and creative practice as real outcomes. Do not solve them by inventing another app unless that is clearly the lowest-friction route. Give weekly behaviors, social commitments, and decision deadlines.

### 7. Project decisions
Issue one verdict for every item: LEAD, DELEGATE, PARTNER, MERGE, PUBLISH, PARK, or KILL. For survivors, define the next proof point, owner, deadline, and maximum additional investment before reevaluation.

### 8. Packaging and publishing plan
Identify reusable IP: frameworks, scoring models, operating models, playbooks, case studies, prototypes, talks, articles, executive one-sheets, and public portfolio pieces. Separate public, internal, and sanitized assets. Prioritize what compounds reputation and opportunity.

### 9. 90-day operating plan
Give me one career bet, one community/life bet, and one creative/commercial bet; weekly milestones; delegated tasks and budget; a 30-minute weekly portfolio review; kill gates; and a visible scoreboard. End with exact first moves for the first seven days.

### 10. The anti-plan
List the tempting avoidance work I am most likely to do—redesigning, adding features, researching tools, creating a new brand, or reopening dead decisions—and tell me what to do when I catch myself doing it.
